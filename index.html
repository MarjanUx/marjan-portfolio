Case study · Mobile app

Västtrafik To-Go Redesign

Roles

UX Researcher, UX/UI Designer, Scrum Master

Methods

  • User survey and interviews
  • Personas
  • Scrum, design thinking
  • Wireframes, user flows, prototype
  • Usability testing

Tools

Figma · Trello

↑ All projects

A redesign of Västtrafik's To-Go app, used to plan journeys and buy tickets for bus, tram and train in Västra Götaland. I found where users got stuck and designed solutions that I tested in prototypes.

  1. EmpathizeWe ran surveys and interviews, and created personas for current users and for people hesitant to use public transport.
  2. DefineUsability tests of the existing app showed four problems: no “Student Ticket” option, unclear rules for bringing a bike, a confusing mix of internal and Google maps, and too much text that made navigation hard.
  3. IdeateWe moved from low-fidelity to mid-fidelity wireframes and then to high-fidelity wireframes.
  4. Prototype & testTesters preferred the redesign but struggled to spot the bike icons. We traced this to the black-and-white mid-fidelity version and added colour in the next round.
  5. Final designTesters gave positive feedback in all three scenarios. We made small changes to colours and navigation flows before presenting.

What I learned

Working in sprints within a team takes structure, and clear communication and shared responsibility make time pressure manageable. I also practised building low-, mid- and high-fidelity wireframes and interactive prototypes in Figma.

Case study · Web design

Småtrollen preschool website

Client

Two representatives from the parents’ council of a parent cooperative preschool

Team & time

Four people, two weeks

Roles

UX Researcher, UX/UI Designer

Methods

  • Heuristic evaluation
  • Competitor analysis
  • Scrum, design thinking
  • Crazy 8 sketching
  • Wireframes, user flows, prototype

Tools

Figma · Trello

↑ All projects

A new mobile website for the preschool Småtrollen, built around usability, easy navigation and minimal maintenance. The client wanted a warm, genuine small-scale feel with lots of nature pictures, a sense of security, and a site that doesn’t need updating for years.

  1. Heuristic evaluationI reviewed the old site against four heuristics: visibility of system status, match between system and reality, consistency and standards, and aesthetics and minimalist design. This showed its flaws and strengths.
  2. IdeateWe used Crazy 8 to produce many sketches quickly and voted on a direction to start from.
  3. Theme, colours and picturesThe old material was not usable, so we used our own photos of the preschool and the children’s activities. Based on the client’s taste, we chose the logo, colours and fonts.
  4. Final prototypeWith only two weeks, we skipped usability testing and went straight to high-fidelity wireframes. We designed some new views and reworked the old ones, using warm colours, more images and easy-to-read information.

What I learned

User testing is essential, and I would test with a focus group if I did this again. I also learned that time planning is harder than expected, and that time pressure can limit creativity.

Case study · UX research

Customer Journey Map: Christmas grocery shopping

Roles

UX Researcher, UX/UI Designer

Methods

  • User survey (17 responses)
  • Four user interviews
  • Observation in store
  • Personas
  • Customer journey map

Tools

Google Forms · Trello

↑ All projects

Research into how people shop for groceries before Christmas, summarised in a customer journey map that shows their frustrations and needs.

  1. SurveyWe collected 17 questionnaire responses in Google Forms to understand the customer journey and the needs behind it.
  2. Interviews & observationThe survey results shaped our interview questions. We held four interviews and followed customers through the store while they did their usual shopping, recording what they did to find patterns.
  3. PersonasThe survey showed who the users in this group were, and we created personas for the different user types.
  4. Journey mapWe mapped customers’ needs and expectations at each stage, identified pain points and pointed out where the experience could be improved.

What I learned

A journey map exposes needs and pain points that can guide later design work. I also learned to organise a UX project with a Trello backlog and time estimates in an agile way.

  2. ProcessI conducted user testing and translated the insights into prototypes in Figma, working agilely with developers and product owners.

Working in a real agile team taught me to turn user insights into solutions that developers and product owners can build on.

My Design Process

01

Discover

User interviews, surveys, and desk research to understand the real problem. I listen before I sketch.

02

Define

Synthesise findings into personas, journey maps, and a clear problem statement that guides every decision.

03

Design

Wireframes and high-fidelity prototypes in Figma, with accessibility (WCAG) baked in from the start.

04

Validate

Usability testing with real users. Iterate based on insights, not assumptions. Collaborate closely with developers.
